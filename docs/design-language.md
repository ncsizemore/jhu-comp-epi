# CIPHER design language

CIPHER presents a research group and its work. Its identity should communicate
careful scientific judgment, intellectual openness, and relevance to public health.

- Use an editorial hierarchy: a clear question, an understandable explanation,
  evidence, then a route into the work. Avoid product slogans, dashboard decoration,
  and numerical claims without a named scope and source.
- Use Newsreader for editorial headings and Geist for body text, navigation,
  captions, and controls. Maintain generous whitespace and a consistent 76rem frame.
- Use ink for reading, Hopkins navy for affiliation/navigation, and pale paper
  backgrounds for section rhythm. The dark research figure is a deliberate focal
  point; its muted gold and teal distinguish scenarios, not product tiers.
- Use thin rules and aligned rows rather than gratuitous cards, gradients, or shadows.
- Show evidence clearly for readers with different technical backgrounds: give the
  question and finding first, label the chart directly, and keep methods/source
  detail available beneath it. Never present model projections as observed data.
- Keep the group collective. People use compact portraits and equivalent treatment;
  contact language is group-neutral, using the current verified contact address.
- Preserve news coverage as a modest editorial section, even with one story.
- Use purposeful interaction, visible focus, native disclosures, and reduced-motion
  support. The desktop header stays available; the phone header scrolls away to
  preserve reading space. No autoplay is needed for the research figure to work.

## Identity

Use the refined parallel-forms symbol with the serif CIPHER wordmark, without
“Lab” in the lockup. In prose, the group remains CIPHER Lab. Artwork is outlined
SVG in `public/brand`, in ink, navy, and white. Use the small symbol for the favicon.
Do not redraw the lettering in a browser font or stretch the artwork. The emblem
is an abstract group identity, not a Johns Hopkins institutional seal.

Fonts are self-hosted; their OFL licenses accompany the assets. The global-aging
analytical interface keeps its existing application typography and controls.
